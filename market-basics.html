Market Basics

What is a financial market?

A financial market is where people and organisations buy and sell assets such as company shares, bonds and currencies. Prices change as buyers and sellers respond to new information, expectations and the wider economy.

Indices and share prices

A share price is the current quoted price for one company’s listed shares. A market index groups selected shares and tracks their combined performance. It gives readers a quick indication of how that group is doing, but it does not describe every company or the whole economy.

For example, the FTSE/JSE Top 40 tracks 40 large companies listed on the JSE. Index values are points, not rand amounts you can directly invest.

Why do markets move?

Prices can respond to company results, interest-rate decisions, inflation, political developments, global events and changes in investor expectations. A market rise or fall is a snapshot of prices at a given time; it is not by itself a forecast or personal investment advice.

Understanding exchange rates

An exchange rate shows how much one currency is worth in another. If the rand weakens against the US dollar, each dollar costs more rand. Currency movements can affect import costs, travel, investments and the rand value of some internationally priced goods.

How to read market data

  • Level: the quoted price or index value.
  • Change: the movement over the stated period, often shown in points and percent.
  • Volume: the number of units traded during a period.
  • Timestamp and status: check whether a quote is live, delayed or a closing value and when it was last updated.
